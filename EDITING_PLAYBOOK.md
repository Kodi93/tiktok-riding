# Zeph Rides — Editing Playbook

## Goal
Make every edit feel deliberate, fast enough for TikTok, recognizably Zeph Rides, and materially different from the repetitive road-POV baseline.

## Frame-zero rule
The first frame must immediately earn attention through one of these:
- unusual movement or encounter;
- obvious visual decision;
- rider/bike hero shot;
- clear detail/startup action;
- strong text tension tied directly to the visual;
- immediate meme/reaction setup.

Avoid slow fades, black frames, camera settling, generic road lead-ins, or text that could apply to any motorcycle clip.

## Timing by format
### 6–10 second shorts
- hook established by 0.0–0.8s;
- first visual change by 1.5s;
- payoff by roughly 4–7s;
- loop or satisfying final beat if possible.

### 10–25 second shorts
- hook by 1s;
- escalate or change visual every 1.5–3s unless the footage itself is compelling;
- payoff should not be saved for the final second if the middle is empty.

### 60–75 second originals
- no padded scenic intro;
- establish the premise inside the first 2s;
- use progression: setup -> development -> turn -> payoff -> close;
- change visual, information, or narrative state frequently enough to justify the length.

## Cut rules
- Cut on action when possible.
- Remove dead steering time unless it creates anticipation.
- Preserve just enough context for real encounters to make sense.
- Do not speed-ramp every clip; use only where the acceleration of information or movement benefits the beat.
- Hard cuts are preferred for comedy and meme reactions.
- Transitions should not call attention to themselves unless the transition is the joke.

## Meme Brain / Reaction Inserts
Use original or rights-safe reaction assets as punctuation, not as the main content.

Recommended structure:
1. setup from real Zeph footage;
2. obvious decision/event beat;
3. 0.35–1.25s reaction insert;
4. immediate return to the real footage payoff.

Default insert timing: 0.5–0.9s.

Good reaction functions:
- chaos approval;
- disbelief;
- self-control failure;
- `terrible idea accepted`;
- wholesome approval;
- `bike brain activated`;
- `road won`;
- `ETA home: eventually`.

Do not let reaction inserts turn the profile into a generic meme page.

## Text
- Keep opening text short enough to read before the visual changes.
- Prefer one thought per card.
- Do not use identical font placement across multiple consecutive posts.
- Use text to sharpen the joke/story, not narrate what is already obvious.
- Avoid generic inspirational filler.

## TTS vs original audio
Use TTS when the joke depends on a clean written setup or inner monologue. Use engine/original audio when the sound itself is satisfying, immersive, or central to the identity.

Do not bury good motorcycle audio under unnecessary music.

## Audio hierarchy
1. intelligible narration/TTS when present;
2. motorcycle/ambient audio when it adds identity;
3. music/sound effect as support;
4. reaction stinger only at the exact punchline beat.

No clipping, blown levels, or abrupt accidental volume jumps.

## Looping
A loop is useful when the final visual naturally feeds back into the first frame. Do not force a loop that makes the story confusing.

## Covers
Every finished edit should have a specific cover decision before scheduling.

Prefer:
- rider/bike hero;
- obvious route choice;
- encounter frame;
- machine detail;
- strong environmental composition;
- reaction-led graphic only when truthful to the content.

Avoid another indistinguishable asphalt frame.

## Edit QA
Before render, ask:
1. Is the first second stronger than the previous version?
2. Can 10–20% be cut without losing the story?
3. Is there a clear payoff?
4. Is the joke understandable muted?
5. Does audio improve the post rather than merely fill silence?
6. Does the cover add profile-grid variety?
7. Is this recognizably Zeph Rides?
8. Is every third-party element rights-safe and necessary?

If the answer to #3, #6, or #8 is no, HOLD.
