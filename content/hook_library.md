# Hook Library

This is a pattern library, not a copy-paste caption bank. The wording must be adapted to the actual clip so the account does not sound automated.

## Hook rule

A strong hook usually does at least one of these:
- creates a question;
- creates tension between expectation and reality;
- points to a specific visual detail;
- starts a story in progress;
- makes a relatable statement that the footage immediately proves;
- presents a real unusual moment before explaining it.

Do not use `POV:` as the default for every post.

## Curiosity hooks

- `Watch what happens after this bend.`
- `I did not expect this road to turn into that.`
- `The GPS left out one detail.`
- `This looked normal for about three seconds.`
- `I almost turned around here.`
- `This is where the route got interesting.`
- `I should have known from the sign.`
- `There is a reason I saved this clip.`

## Encounter hooks

- `I thought he was mad at me.`
- `This made the whole ride.`
- `I was not expecting a fan club today.`
- `The road had other plans.`
- `This is why the camera stays running.`
- `I almost missed this.`
- `Best reaction I got all day.`
- `Nobody warned me about traffic like this.`

## Humor / rider-life hooks

- `Quick ride. Famous last words.`
- `I said I would be home by six.`
- `One more corner is not a unit of time.`
- `The GPS said two minutes.`
- `This was supposed to be relaxing.`
- `Motorcycle math is different.`
- `I definitely meant to take this route.`
- `Technically, I am still on my way home.`

## Road / route hooks

- `WV road test: this one gets weird.`
- `This road starts innocent.`
- `The centerline should have been a warning.`
- `This is why I stopped taking the shortest route.`
- `The map does not show you this part.`
- `Worth the detour?`
- `This corner hides another one.`
- `The road just keeps going.`

## Rider / identity hooks

- `Helmet on. Everything else off for an hour.`
- `This is the part nobody sees before the ride.`
- `No destination today.`
- `Some days the bike is the plan.`
- `This is my version of disappearing for a minute.`
- `No face reveal. The bike gets the screen time.`

Use identity hooks sparingly; do not drift into generic motivational filler.

## Machine/detail hooks

- `The weirdest thing about this bike is not what people think.`
- `This is the detail everyone asks about.`
- `You hear it before you notice it.`
- `This startup never gets old.`
- `The cockpit is half the experience.`
- `This is where the hybrid part gets interesting.`

Verify technical claims before publishing factual content.

## Story openings

- `About ten minutes earlier, I almost took the other road.`
- `This ride was supposed to be completely uneventful.`
- `I only noticed him when he pulled alongside.`
- `I had been following this road for twenty minutes when...`
- `The plan was simple until the route changed.`

## Visual-first / no-text hooks

Not every post needs opening text. Favor no-text when frame zero already contains:
- an animal in/near the roadway;
- a strong rider/bike hero composition;
- an unusual road feature;
- another rider/person reacting;
- a dramatic close-up/startup;
- an immediate movement/reveal that is obvious without explanation.

## Anti-repetition rules

Across any six consecutive posts:
- do not begin more than two with the same grammatical structure;
- do not use `POV:` more than once unless a deliberate A/B test is running;
- do not repeat the same punchline concept;
- do not use `the problem with...`, `some people need...`, or similar reflective-template language repeatedly;
- do not use generic inspirational hooks as filler;
- do not place every hook in the same screen position.

## Hook QA

Before approving a hook, ask:
1. Could this line apply to 10,000 unrelated motorcycle clips? If yes, make it more specific.
2. Does the visual support the claim immediately?
3. Does the viewer need the next second to resolve something?
4. Is the wording natural enough that a real rider might say it?
5. Is this materially different from the last few posts?
