# Zeph Rides — Shoot Day SOP

## Purpose
Every ride should return with enough varied footage to support professional short-form edits, covers, meme reactions, longer originals, and future series without over-relying on forward road POV.

## Non-negotiable capture rule
Do not finish a ride with only helmet/chest road footage. Every shoot day should intentionally collect rider, bike, environment, detail, and narrative beats.

## Before leaving
- clean lens and visor;
- verify storage, battery, camera angle, stabilization, and audio;
- confirm date/route label for source inventory;
- capture one clean bike hero still/video before departure;
- capture one rider + bike hero shot with helmet on;
- capture startup/cluster/controls;
- capture glove-on / key / ignition / mount sequence;
- capture a clean 2–4 second stationary establishing shot.

## Minimum capture set per ride
Target at least:
- 3 rider/bike hero shots;
- 3 machine/detail shots;
- 3 environmental/location shots;
- 2 mount/dismount or walk-up shots;
- 2 startup/control clips;
- 2 route-decision moments, intersections, forks, signs, pull-offs, or obvious visual choices;
- 2 dedicated cover candidates framed vertically;
- 1 intentionally funny or relatable rider-life beat;
- 1 quiet/cinematic beat;
- 1 usable 45–90 second continuous segment for a future one-minute original;
- all authentic unusual encounters preserved in full with context.

## Priority narrative footage
Always preserve footage that contains a clear story decision or event. Examples:
- hard left off the main road onto a back road;
- unexpected animal crossing;
- another rider/driver/person reacting;
- wrong turn or scenic detour;
- weather shift;
- road suddenly changes character;
- unusual sign/bridge/road feature;
- fuel stop or mundane reality that contrasts with polished riding edits.

Do not trim or overwrite the seconds immediately before and after these moments. Context is editorially valuable.

## Cover capture discipline
For every strong location or narrative beat, ask: `Could this become a profile-grid cover?`

Capture at least one of:
- rider + bike separated from background;
- bike detail with clear focal point;
- obvious road choice/fork/turn;
- scenic pull-off with depth;
- unusual encounter frame;
- silhouette/night/lighting frame;
- readable sign or distinctive road feature.

Do not rely on extracting every cover from generic asphalt footage.

## Meme-reaction capture support
When a moment visually communicates a decision, mistake, temptation, surprise, or approval, preserve a clean setup and payoff around it. These are ideal for `Meme Brain / Reaction Inserts`.

Examples:
- main road -> tempting back road -> commit to turn;
- `one more corner` -> another road appears;
- gas stop -> immediately leaving again;
- route says home -> rider chooses detour;
- weather looks fine -> instant weather shift.

## Safety / integrity
Content capture never justifies unsafe riding. Do not stage dangerous maneuvers, manufacture near-misses, or create events that did not happen. If a shot requires stopping, stop somewhere legal and safe before filming.

## After the ride
Within 24 hours:
1. ingest footage into a dated ride folder;
2. preserve original files unchanged;
3. log source filenames in `content/source_inventory.csv`;
4. mark standout timestamps for encounters, turns, reactions, and unusual visuals;
5. identify at least 3 cover candidates;
6. identify at least 3 short-form concepts;
7. identify whether the ride contains a viable 60+ second original;
8. flag any clip that could support a meme-reaction edit;
9. back up before deleting anything from cards/devices.

## Shoot-day success test
A successful ride returns enough material that the next 6–9 posts could include several different visual families without looking like the same road clip repeated.
